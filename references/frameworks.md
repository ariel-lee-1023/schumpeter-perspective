# Named constructs, in Schumpeter's own senses

His terminology is load-bearing and not interchangeable with paraphrase. Where he defines a
term narrowly against ordinary usage, the narrowness *is* the argument. Cluster tags in
brackets point to the cluster file carrying the evidence.

---

## Creative Destruction  [c04–c08]

The process of industrial mutation that incessantly revolutionizes the economic structure
**from within**, incessantly destroying the old one and creating a new one. Not a side effect
of capitalism and not a cost of it — "the essential fact about capitalism," what capitalism
consists in and what every capitalist concern has to live in.

Two inferences he always draws from it, in this order:
1. **No appraisal *ex visu* of a point of time.** Performance must be judged as the process
   unfolds over decades or centuries. A system that at every instant fully uses its
   possibilities may still lose over the long run to one that never does.
2. **No isolated evaluation of a business practice.** Every piece of business strategy takes
   its meaning from the process and the situation the process created. Analysing an
   individual firm or industry clarifies mechanism and is inconclusive beyond that.

Companion image: **the perennial gale.** The error he attacks is the assumption of a perennial
lull — treating the momentary data as if there were no past or future to them.

## Innovation, and its distinction from invention  [c01]

**Innovation** = the setting up of a new production function: new goods, new methods, new
markets, new sources of supply, new forms of organization. Defined also in cost terms: where
a given output costs less than the same or a smaller output cost before, factor prices not
having fallen, there has been innovation somewhere.

The **invention** is a different act from the **innovation**. The two are often performed by
the same person, but that is a chance coincidence which does not affect the validity of the
distinction; the aptitudes differ (the inventor's primarily intellectual). He correspondingly
distrusts the category of "revolutionary inventions" as a distinct class.

**Economic Evolution** = the changes brought about by innovation, together with all their
effects and the system's response to them. He chose the word deliberately over *progress*,
to avoid the complacency that term implies.

## The entrepreneurial function  [c01, c04–c08]

Carrying out innovations — "getting things done" — and nothing else. What it is *not*:

| Not | Because |
|---|---|
| the inventor | making and carrying out are different acts |
| the capitalist | "It is leadership rather than ownership that matters" |
| the risk-bearer | the capitalist bears the risk; riskiness enters only as environmental resistance |
| the manager | deciding how much wool to buy is not introducing a new process |
| a permanent status | nobody is ever an entrepreneur all the time, and nobody can ever be only an entrepreneur |

Failing to see this as a distinct function *sui generis* he calls the common fault of the
classics **and** of Marx.

**Horizon** — the range of choice within which a businessman moves freely and within which
his decision can be described purely in terms of profitability and foresight. The
entrepreneur's distinguishing capacity is deciding *outside* the horizon: choosing among
untried possibilities, not merely among tried ones. This capacity is distributed across the
population on something like a skew normal law — it is not confined to a few great men.

Three sources of the difficulty of doing something new: the environment resists (disapproval,
prohibition, machine-breaking); the environment withholds the prerequisites (lenders lend
readily for routine, customers buy what they understand); and most people feel an inhibition,
part rational and part not.

## Credit creation  [c01]

Innovation is financed not out of prior saving but out of **created** means of payment — a
bank balance whose creation increases the total of existing means of payment. What innovation
requires is not new factors but the **shifting of existing factors from old to new uses**;
created credit is the capitalist method of effecting that shift, corresponding to the
socialist community's cancelling of an old order and issuing of a new one. Interest is
sourced in entrepreneurial profit; in perfect equilibrium pure interest tends to vanish.

## The three-cycle schema  [c01–c03]

Kitchin, Juglar, Kondratieff, superimposed. Its status matters as much as its content: adopting
it "implies the refusal to accept" the single-cycle hypothesis without erecting another
hypothesis in its place. It is **a decision, not a hypothesis**, and he waives every claim
beyond the ones he states. He assigns the first Kondratieff to the Industrial Revolution and
its absorption (1780s–1842), the second to railroadization, the third from the 1890s.

Cycles are not a pathology superimposed on growth; they are the **form** economic evolution
takes. Evolution is "lopsided, discontinuous, disharmonious by nature" — more like a series of
explosions than a gentle transformation. Innovations **cluster** because success makes
imitation easier, and cluster **sectorally**, so one can locate the ignition of each process
in particular industries and particular firms.

## Equilibrium, and the internal/external distinction  [c01]

Equilibrium is the theoretical norm — the state in which the data and the relations uniquely
determine prices and quantities. Its value is as a benchmark against which to identify what the
system generates from within. **External factors** (war, policy change, institutional change,
harvests) may explain a great deal and might conceivably explain everything, but a theory
resting on them explains no mechanism. The analytic prize is the **internal** factor. His
biological analogy: understanding the circulation of the blood in a dog tells you nothing about
how such things as dogs came to exist at all.

## Vision, and the ubiquity of ideological bias  [c09–c10]

**Vision** = the pre-analytic cognitive act that supplies the raw material for analysis. It
must precede analytic effort, and it re-enters an established science whenever someone teaches
us to *see* things in a light not derivable from the existing state of the science. Vision is
"ideological almost by definition."

**Ideology** (his amended Marxian sense) = a system of ideas erected on, and produced by, the
realities of social structure below it, which tends to reflect those realities in a
characteristically biased manner. He widens Marx's version — social location shapes minds, but
not exclusively through economic class *interest*.

Three corrections he makes to Marx on this, and they are the operative rules:
1. **No exemption.** The principle is perfectly general; there is no "everywhere else is
   ideology, we alone stand on the rock of absolute truth." (Applied against Mannheim's
   free-floating intelligentsia — the "fire escape" he refuses.)
2. **Not reducible to economic class interest** alone.
3. **Ideologies are not lies.** Statements displaying ideological influence are not thereby
   condemned; explaining why a man says something says nothing about its truth.

The **rules of procedure** are what grind ideology out — automatically, slowly, incompletely,
and irrespective of the researcher's wishes. Note also the asymmetry he insists on: love
distorts, hate distorts more.

Distinguish sharply, and he does so constantly:
- **ideological bias** — deflected vision;
- **value judgment** — may *reveal* an ideology but is not one;
- **special pleading** — tampering with facts or procedure; a different offence entirely.

## The three (four) fundamental fields  [c09]

**Economic history · statistics and statistical method · theory** — together, Economic
Analysis; plus **economic sociology** as a fourth. History is "by far the most important," and
if allowed only one he takes it. His three grounds: the subject matter is a unique process in
historic time; the historical report is unavoidably institutional and so shows how the social
sciences *should* relate; and most fundamental errors in economic analysis come from want of
historical experience.

Corollary he presses hard: the techniques of the neighbouring field are part of *our* tool
box. "Latin palaeography, for instance, is one of the techniques of economic analysis."

## Analytic progress vs. the meaninglessness of progress in policy  [c09]

**History of economic analysis** ≠ **history of systems of political economy** (advocacy on
unifying normative principles) ≠ **history of economic thought** (the opinions floating in the
public mind). Only the first admits of progress, because only there is a professional standard
that arrays performances in a series. Among policy systems there is no valid standard of
interpersonal comparison — hence Gauguin-or-Titian, hence blondes-or-brunettes.

The reconciliation he offers: those who see analytic progress are right, those who deny
progress in economic thought are right, and each errs only in overlooking the other's half.

## Imperialism as atavism  [c11]

**Definition:** "imperialism is the objectless disposition on the part of a state to unlimited
forcible expansion." *Objectless* is the operative word — such expansion requires concrete
objects to reach the action stage but has no adequate object beyond itself, and therefore is
never satisfied by attaining one; hence its tendency to transcend all bounds to the point of
exhaustion. Where the theory of concrete interest fails is exactly where the will to victory
itself has to be explained.

**Atavism:** a survival from earlier living conditions and relations of production, lodged in
social structure and in habitual emotional reaction. It must gradually disappear as the
structure that produced it declines and as rationalization absorbs the old functional needs
into new tasks — though any warlike involvement revives it. Testable prediction he draws:
imperialisms should decline in intensity the later they occur in a people's history.

**The war machine that outlives its purpose:** created by the wars that required it, it now
creates the wars it requires. The professional warrior stratum's social position depends on
war continuing, and its ideology (honour, faith, mission) is superstructure on that.

## Social classes  [c12]

- **Class position rests on function**, not on wealth. Wealth is symptom and consequence.
  Becoming an entrepreneur does not of itself constitute class position.
- **A class rises and falls with the vitality of its function** and with its success in
  discharging it — not with the individual merits of its current members.
- **The hotel or omnibus:** for the duration of a class's collective life, it is always full
  and always of different people. Families enter and leave individually, and that individual
  movement is itself a class process.
- Rising by **striking out along unconventional paths** has always mattered, and never so much
  as under capitalism.
- Classes erect legal and other barriers to slow the interchange, for obvious reasons.

## The tax state  [c13]

**Fiscal sociology.** The budget is the skeleton of the state stripped of all misleading
ideologies (Goldscheid's phrase, adopted). Fiscal history is causally decisive — fiscal motives
alone shaped the economic policy of Charles V, the foreign-merchant domination of England, the
Colbertian guild order, the Great Elector's settlement of French artisans — and, more
importantly still, **symptomatically** decisive: the spirit of a people, its cultural level,
its social structure and the deeds its policy may prepare are all written there, stripped of
all phrases. He who knows how to listen discerns the thunder of world history more clearly
there than anywhere else.

The **tax state** is a historically specific form, born of a specific crisis, with a limit: it
can take only so much before it destroys the private motives on which the private economy
runs. His verdict on the post-1918 fiscal crisis is characteristically split — the tax state
is not at the end of its rope from the war burden, and yet society is growing beyond private
enterprise and the tax state anyway, *not because but in spite of* the war.

## The classical doctrine of democracy, and the competitive theory  [c07 within c04–c08]

**Classical doctrine (rejected):** the democratic method as the institutional arrangement that
realizes the common good by making the people decide issues through elected representatives.
It fails on both legs — there is no univocal common good on which all reasonably could agree,
and the "will of the people" is largely manufactured, since the typical citizen drops to a
lower level of mental performance the moment he enters the political field.

**His definition (the competitive theory):** "the democratic method is that institutional
arrangement for arriving at political decisions in which individuals acquire the power to
decide by means of a competitive struggle for the people's vote." Selection of issues becomes
secondary to selection of the government. Its advantage is that it supplies a verifiable
*modus procedendi* by which to tell democracies from other regimes.

## The decay thesis  [c04–c08]

Capitalism's success, not its failure, is fatal, along four converging lines:
1. **Obsolescence of the entrepreneurial function** — innovation reduced to routine, handled
   by teams of trained specialists; progress depersonalized and automatized.
2. **Destruction of the protecting strata** — the bourgeoisie is rationalist and unheroic,
   politically incapable, and has destroyed the pre-capitalist strata that shielded it.
3. **Destruction of the institutional framework** — the erosion of property and free contract
   as living realities in the large corporation ("the evaporation of the substance of
   property").
4. **Growing hostility** — a large intellectual stratum, produced by capitalism itself, that
   lives on criticism which stings and cannot help nibbling.

The verdict form he uses: not breakdown under economic failure, but success undermining the
social institutions that protect it, pointing to socialism as the heir apparent.
